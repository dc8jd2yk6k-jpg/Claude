# The $80 Billion Paradox — narration script

Independent explainer. Figures come from press reports of Anthropic’s **draft, unaudited** S-1 and related coverage (Sep–Oct 2026) and may change. Not affiliated with Anthropic. Not investment advice.

Timestamps are from `build/timeline.json` (the cut the visuals, score and mix are built on). Bracketed numbers point to sources below.


## Cold open

- `0:03.00` In May 2026, the CEO of Anthropic said something almost no CEO ever gets to say.
- `0:10.24` “We tried to plan very well for a world of 10x growth per year. And yet we saw 80x.” [5]
- `0:17.55` Five months later, the same company revealed this number.
- `0:21.99` A loss of $80.7 billion. In six months. [1]
- `0:26.79` But before we get to that number, you need to understand just how strong this company really is.

## Chapter 1 — The strongest company in AI

- `0:38.00` Exhibit one: speed.
- `0:40.28` At the start of 2025, Anthropic’s revenue was running at about $1 billion a year. [7]
- `0:46.87` By the end of the year: $9 billion. [5]
- `0:49.48` February: $14 billion. April: $30 billion. May: $47 billion. [7][5][9]
- `0:53.81` And by the end of July, a run rate of $65 billion a year. [6]
- `0:59.10` Second-quarter revenue alone topped $11.5 billion — nearly 15 times the same quarter a year earlier. [8]
- `1:08.00` Exhibit two: it overtook the leader.
- `1:10.82` For years, OpenAI was the undisputed king of AI revenue. By August, Anthropic’s run rate was reported at $65 billion. OpenAI’s: around $40 billion. [6]
- `1:22.92` In May, investors valued Anthropic at $965 billion — above OpenAI’s last price tag of $852 billion. [9]
- `1:36.00` Exhibit three: business runs on it.
- `1:38.99` In a survey by Menlo Ventures — itself an Anthropic investor — Anthropic took 40% of enterprise spending on AI models. OpenAI: 27%. Google: 21%. [10]
- `1:51.52` In coding, its share was 54%. [10]
- `1:54.57` Customers paying more than $1 million a year doubled — from 500 to over 1,000 — in less than two months. [11]
- `2:02.32` And its coding agent, Claude Code, became a multi-billion-dollar business almost overnight. [11]
- `2:10.00` Exhibit four: the giants pay to be in the room.
- `2:13.31` Amazon. Google. Microsoft. Nvidia. All four are investors. [13][12]
- `2:18.25` Amazon put in about $13 billion. By June, it valued that stake at roughly $190 billion. [12]
- `2:25.97` And the compute is staggering: up to a million Google TPUs, Amazon’s Project Rainier, a gigawatt on Microsoft Azure. [13]
- `2:36.00` Exhibit five: it’s starting to make money.
- `2:38.91` In both of the first two quarters of 2026, Anthropic reported positive adjusted operating income. [3]
- `2:48.00` Put it all together, and you get one of the fastest climbs in corporate history.
- `2:52.63` March 2025: $61.5 billion. [21]
- `2:56.22` September: $183 billion. [22]
- `2:58.18` February 2026: $380 billion. [14]
- `3:00.07` May: $965 billion. [9]
- `3:03.07` And now, talk of an IPO at up to $2 trillion. [20]

## Chapter 2 — The leak

- `3:12.00` Then came the draft IPO filing — and three numbers that made Wall Street do a double take.
- `3:18.24` Revenue, first half of 2026: $16.3 billion. [2]
- `3:24.11` Net loss: $80.7 billion. [1]
- `3:27.83` Cash and short-term investments at the end of 2025: $20.28 billion. [4]
- `3:34.49` Now do the scary math.
- `3:36.36` $80.7 billion in six months is about $13.5 billion a month.
- `3:41.93` Twenty billion in the bank runs out in about 45 days.
- `3:46.39` By that logic, the strongest company in AI should have been dead by Valentine’s Day.
- `3:52.69` So which is it? A rocket… or a bomb?

## Chapter 3 — Plot twist #1: the phantom loss

- `4:00.00` Let’s take that $80.7 billion apart.
- `4:03.24` About $72 billion of it is a single accounting line. And not one dollar of it left the company. [1]
- `4:10.21` It comes from convertible notes: money investors put in that can later turn into shares.
- `4:16.18` Imagine a friend lends you $10. Instead of paying it back in cash, you promise shares of your company, at today’s price.
- `4:24.19` Then your company becomes ten times more valuable.
- `4:27.27` Those shares are now worth $100. On paper, you owe your friend $100.
- `4:32.49` Accounting rules say that $90 jump is a loss. You didn’t spend a cent.
- `4:38.01` Now scale it up. Anthropic’s valuation went from $380 billion to $965 billion in about three months. [14][9]
- `4:47.21` Every leap in value made those notes more expensive — and the reported loss bigger.
- `4:52.58` In other words: the stronger Anthropic gets, the more money it appears to lose.
- `4:58.03` You can see the other side of this trade on Amazon’s books.
- `5:01.79` At the end of March, Amazon valued its Anthropic convertible notes at $42.2 billion. By the end of June: $97.9 billion. [12][20]
- `5:11.63` A $55.7 billion gain in a single quarter. Same notes. One company books a gain. The other, a loss. [20]
- `5:20.49` And it isn’t new. Of Anthropic’s roughly $42 billion net loss in 2025, about $34 billion was the same kind of charge. [3]
- `5:30.27` Strip it out, and the $80 billion crater shrinks to under $9 billion.
- `5:34.68` So the loss is fake?

## Chapter 4 — Plot twist #2: the real bill

- `5:38.00` Not so fast.
- `5:39.63` Because there’s a number in the filing that is very, very real.
- `5:43.79` Compute. In the first half of 2025, Anthropic spent $2.3 billion on computing and infrastructure. A year later: nearly $9 billion. [1]
- `5:54.75` And that’s just the beginning. The filing reportedly lists at least $518 billion of AI-infrastructure commitments over roughly the next decade. [15]
- `6:05.22` Google: $111 billion. [15]
- `6:07.79` Amazon: $110 billion. [15]
- `6:10.41` Microsoft: $31 billion. [15]
- `6:12.72` Nvidia GPUs through smaller cloud providers: about $86 billion. [16]
- `6:17.38` And equipment leases tied to Broadcom: $161 billion. [15]
- `6:22.25` Roughly 80% of it can’t be cancelled. [15]
- `6:25.58` Now put the cash next to it.
- `6:28.53` $20.28 billion. The bill is about 25 times bigger. [4]
- `6:34.15` To be fair, that cash figure is a December snapshot. In 2026, Anthropic announced two more rounds: $30 billion in February, $65 billion in May. [14][9]
- `6:45.17` Even so, that’s a fraction of the bill.
- `6:48.22` And look at who sits on both sides of the table.
- `6:51.34` Amazon and Google invest in Anthropic. Anthropic pays them for cloud. Their stakes rise in value — and Anthropic books a loss.
- `7:00.43` The filing even warns that its cloud partners increasingly see Anthropic as a competitor. [18]
- `7:06.67` And roughly a fifth to a quarter of its revenue came from just two customers. [17]

## Chapter 5 — Strong or fragile?

- `7:14.00` So: is Anthropic strong, or fragile?
- `7:17.20` It depends on which lens you use.
- `7:19.49` Lens one: the income statement. Here, success looks like failure — the higher the valuation, the bigger the paper loss.
- `7:27.19` Lens two: operations. Here, the engine is roaring. Revenue is compounding, and adjusted operating income has turned positive.
- `7:36.54` Lens three: commitments. Here is the real risk — a half-trillion-dollar bill that comes due whether customers show up or not.
- `7:45.01` The bulls see a company growing faster than almost anything in history, with demand that still outruns supply.
- `7:51.94` The bears see a fixed bill, concentrated customers, and suppliers who are also rivals. One research firm says the whole company is worth just $144 billion. [19]
- `8:04.23` And that is exactly why Anthropic is going public. To keep paying for the future, it needs the deepest pool of capital on Earth. [1]

## Outro — the verdict

- `8:16.00` So let’s look at those three numbers one more time.
- `8:19.31` $16.3 billion in revenue: real demand.
- `8:23.25` $80.7 billion in losses: mostly a side effect of success.
- `8:28.64` $20.28 billion in cash: a snapshot, not a runway.
- `8:33.97` Anthropic’s strength is real. So is the bill.
- `8:37.54` The only question is which one grows faster.

- Run-rates are company-reported annualizations; Anthropic and OpenAI may calculate them differently.
