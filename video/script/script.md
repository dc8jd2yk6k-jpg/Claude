# The $80 Billion Paradox — narration script (v2)

Independent explainer. Figures come from press reports of Anthropic’s **draft, unaudited** S-1 and related coverage (Sep–Oct 2026) and may change. Not affiliated with Anthropic. Not investment advice.

Timestamps come from `build/timeline.json`. Bracketed numbers point to the sources below.


## Hook

- `0:00.35` Anthropic just lost $80.7 billion. In six months. [1]
- `0:04.67` And yet, investors want to value it at up to $2 trillion. [6]
- `0:08.54` One of these numbers is mostly an illusion. And the number that could really sink it isn’t on screen yet.

## 1 · How strong is it?

- `0:15.27` First: how strong is Anthropic, really?
- `0:17.70` At the start of 2025, its revenue was running at about $1 billion a year. By July 2026: $65 billion. [7][6]
- `0:26.31` Its CEO said they planned for 10x growth, and got 80x. [5]
- `0:30.07` That’s ahead of OpenAI, reportedly at around $40 billion. [6]
- `0:33.91` One survey, by Menlo Ventures, an Anthropic investor, gave it 40% of enterprise AI-model spending, and 54% of coding. [9]
- `0:43.30` Amazon put in about $13 billion. That stake is now marked at roughly $190 billion. [10]
- `0:49.75` Its valuation? $61.5 billion in March 2025. [11]
- `0:54.12` $965 billion this May. Now, IPO talk of up to $2 trillion. [8][6]

## 2 · The leak

- `1:01.09` Then the draft IPO filing leaked, with three numbers that stunned Wall Street.
- `1:05.54` Revenue, first half of 2026: $16.3 billion. [2]
- `1:10.09` Net loss: $80.7 billion. [1]
- `1:12.56` Cash and short-term investments, end of 2025: $20.28 billion. [4]
- `1:17.97` That loss is $13.5 billion a month. At that rate, twenty billion lasts about 45 days.
- `1:24.61` Dead by Valentine’s Day. So… why isn’t it?

## 3 · Twist #1: the phantom loss

- `1:29.45` Because about $72 billion of that loss never left the building. [1]
- `1:33.15` It’s an accounting charge on convertible notes: money investors lent that turns into shares later.
- `1:39.22` Picture it: a friend lends you $10, and you promise to repay in your company’s shares, at today’s price.
- `1:45.47` Your company becomes ten times more valuable. Those shares are now worth $100.
- `1:50.58` On paper, you just lost $90. In cash, you spent nothing.
- `1:54.72` Anthropic’s valuation jumped about two and a half times in three months. The notes got pricier, and the reported loss exploded. [12][8]
- `2:02.83` The stronger Anthropic gets, the more money it appears to lose.
- `2:06.48` The proof: that same quarter, Amazon marked those very notes up by $55.7 billion. [10][18]
- `2:12.99` Strip the charge out, and the $80 billion loss shrinks to under $9 billion. [1]
- `2:17.28` So the loss is fake?

## 4 · Twist #2: the real bill

- `2:19.64` Not so fast. There’s a number in the filing that is very, very real.
- `2:23.51` Compute. Its half-year compute bill went from $2.3 billion to nearly $9 billion. [1]
- `2:29.40` And the filing reportedly lists at least $518 billion in AI-infrastructure commitments over roughly a decade. [13]
- `2:37.07` Google. Amazon. Microsoft. Nvidia chips. Broadcom-linked leases. [13][14]
- `2:41.77` About 80% of it can’t be cancelled. [13]
- `2:44.31` Now put the cash next to it. The bill is about 25 times bigger. [4]
- `2:48.40` Yes, that cash figure is from December, and $95 billion in new funding has been announced since. Still a fraction of the bill. [12][8]
- `2:56.54` Its compute landlords are also its investors, and, the filing warns, increasingly its rivals. [15]
- `3:02.56` And roughly a fifth to a quarter of revenue comes from just two customers. [16]

## 5 · The verdict

- `3:07.64` So: strong, or fragile? Valuations range from $144 billion to $2 trillion. [17][8][6]
- `3:14.20` Look at those three numbers again.
- `3:15.87` $16.3 billion in revenue: real demand.
- `3:19.14` $80.7 billion in losses: mostly the price of success.
- `3:23.39` $20.28 billion in cash: a snapshot, not a runway.
- `3:27.47` That’s why it’s going public. It needs the deepest pool of capital on Earth. [1]
- `3:31.66` Anthropic’s strength is real. So is the bill. The only question is which one grows faster.

### Where reports disagree

- Two-customer concentration: ~20% (Reuters) vs ~25% (Inc.) — the video says “a fifth to a quarter”.
- Total compute commitments: ≥$518B (Reuters) vs ~$450B in cloud agreements and up to $750B including deals under negotiation (WSJ). The $86B Nvidia/neocloud figure comes from a separate report, so components may not sum exactly.
- Adjusted operating income is company-defined; run-rates are company-reported annualizations and Anthropic and OpenAI may calculate them differently.
- The 2026 funding figure ($95B) is the headline size of two announced rounds ($30B in February, $65B in May, which included $15B previously committed by hyperscalers); it is not a cash balance.
